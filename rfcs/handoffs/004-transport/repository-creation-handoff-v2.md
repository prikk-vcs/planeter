## T0 — Measure first (the lesson of part 4b: no cost constant without a measurement)

In your own scratch area with real prikk 0.54.1 (as in part 4b: the real call, a data-segment sampler, no ceiling and then a ceiling): `archive verify` and `archive import --adopt` of archives of these shapes — 1,000 / 5,000 patches of one file each; 100 × 1 MiB files in one patch; **one large blob** of 10, 50, 100 MiB (random); a file edited 50 times (4 MiB); a 70-block history — report peak `VmData`, resident set, wall time (host load noted), archive bytes, and whether `--adopt` is refused or lands under `remotes/` and why. Fit `peak ≈ a + b × archive_bytes + c × objects`, give the worst ratio per shape, and say which bounds prikk itself enforces for archives (environment variables or flags; look in `prikk --help` and its source in the cargo registry; `PRIKK_BUNDLE_MAX_*` are named in RFC 004 D-5.2). Propose the cost rule for `ReadVerb`-style admission of `archive verify` (R) and `archive import` (W) and the default bounds; **implement the rule in one place with its provenance**, like `cost_model` in `propose.rs`. Until you have the table, nothing else in this handoff may use a guessed constant.

## Change scope

- **T1 — The record and the kill-safe creation.** A repository record gets a state `creating | ready` (migration, idempotent; existing rows are `ready`). A `creating` repository is **invisible and unroutable** everywhere (the read gate answers the existence-neutral 404 exactly as for an absent one; the registry's probe covers it) and never counted in listings or quotas of others. `create_repo` becomes: reserve the name (unique, checked before any byte is written) as `creating` → lay out and `prikk init` → … → mark `ready` as the **last** step. **Any failure, timeout or kill before `ready` removes the repository whole** (directory and record, in that order; a record without a directory or a directory without a record is repaired at start-up: the start-up sweep deletes every `creating` repository's directory and record, and every directory under the repository root that has no record, **logging** what it removed — and refusing to delete anything outside the repository root, with a test for a symbolic link planted in it). Tests: a kill (SIGKILL of the real server) in the middle of an archive creation leaves nothing visible and the next start leaves nothing on disk; the name is free again; an injected failure at every step removes everything.
- **T2 — Create an empty repository (API and page).** `POST /api/v1/repos` `{owner, name, visibility, proposals?}` (bearer only; `proposals` defaults to `members`, the `any-authenticated` choice is allowed to the creator only with the page's plain explanation) and a page `/new`. Authorization: the principal creates in **their own namespace**, or in an organization where `authorize(CreateRepo)` allows; anything else is the neutral refusal (no organization-existence oracle). Name rules are the existing validation plus a reserved-names list in one place (`new`, `api`, `login`, `static`, … whatever the router already owns — derive it from the router, with a test that fails when a route is added that a name could shadow); a taken name answers `409 name_taken` **only to someone allowed to create there**. A **quota** of repositories per owner (operator variable, defaults 50 for a user and 500 for an organization) with a named refusal and catalog sentence. The creator becomes the repository's administrator by the existing grant model. Rate limit per principal (10 creations an hour).
- **T3 — Stage an archive (step 1).** `POST /api/v1/repos/import` (bearer only): the propose route's framing (magic line `PLANETER-IMPORT-1\n`, 8-byte big-endian metadata length ≤ 64 KiB, JSON metadata `{owner, name, visibility}`, then the archive; `Content-Length` required; size cap before the first byte is read; streamed to disk and hashed; idle and total cut-offs). The edge authorization and checks of T2 run **before the body is read**. The archive is written to a per-stage working area **outside every repository**, then `prikk archive verify --format json` runs in the sandbox against a fresh scratch `init`-ed repository (R/W lease classes do not apply: the repository does not exist yet; use the admission of T0 and the sandbox mount rules of the propose accept — read-only archive, nothing else mounted). The response (`201`) is a **stage**: id, expiry (24 h), the archive's size and digest, what `verify` found (internally consistent or the refusal text), the **carried maintainer keys** (key id, full fingerprint, public key), the refs it carries and, per ref, whether `--adopt` could publish it (absent locally, signer among the carried keys) and why not otherwise, patch/object counts. An archive that fails `verify` is refused (`422`) with prikk's text behind our sentence and **nothing is kept**. Stages: at most 2 per principal, a total byte budget (operator variable), expiry sweep (start-up and hourly), the staged file is **never served back**.
- **T4 — Confirm (step 2).** `POST /api/v1/repos/import/{stage}/confirm` `{adopt: [{key_id, fingerprint}], no_adopt?: true}` by the principal who staged it (any other caller: the neutral 404). **Adoption is the creator's act: the fingerprints must be typed or pasted by the creator, never prefilled** — the page's form has the carried keys listed for comparison and **empty input fields**; each `fingerprint` must equal (case-insensitive, whole) the carried key's fingerprint, every carried key must be either adopted (matched) or explicitly left out, and a mismatch is refused naming which key and showing nothing prefilled. `no_adopt: true` creates the repository with everything under `remotes/`, and the response and the page say in one sentence that **nothing is verified or trusted until a key is adopted** (RFC 004 Q-2). The sequence, as one operation under the new repository's **W lease** with its own deadline (600 s default, an operator variable) and memory admission from T0: mark `creating` → `init` → for each adopted key `trust maintainer add --key-id --public-key` → `archive import --adopt` → fresh `verify` (must exit 0 when keys were adopted; its failure removes the repository) → `ready` → remove the stage. The result lists, per ref, whether it landed as a published branch or under `remotes/`, with prikk's reason, and the next step in plain words. All-or-nothing as in T1; the second confirm of one stage is refused; an expired stage is `410`.
- **T5 — Pages and words.** `/new` offers two plain choices — *Empty repository* and *From an archive a maintainer gave you* — and the archive path is a two-screen flow (upload and see what it contains; confirm the keys by typing their fingerprints), each screen stating its consequence beside the button; no script required; keyboard reachable; the structural checker, plain-words and stylesheet tests in both languages; the key fingerprints are shown in groups for reading and copyable. Glossary rows drafted for the architect (*archive*, *maintainer key*, *fingerprint*, *trust a key* — the plain word for prikk's *adopt*; *staged*). Catalog messages in both languages; Japanese draft rows in an addendum.
- **T6 — Documentation and operations.** `docs/creation.md` (the two paths, the stage lifecycle, what is trusted when, the kill behaviour, the settings), the runbook for the start-up sweep, CHANGELOG, STATUS. The architect updates the threat model.

## Non-change scope

No push/landing of newer history into an existing repository (decision D-8), no import of a second archive, no rename/transfer/delete of repositories, no organization or account management, no SSH, no client helper, no change to `propose`, recovery or review, no new dependency, no `unsafe`, no version or release change.

## Acceptance criteria

T0's table and rule; the empty creation end to end (own namespace, org member allowed, stranger refused neutrally, name taken only to the allowed, quota, reserved names, rate limit); the archive path end to end with **real prikk** (a maintainer's archive made with a keyed prikk: stage → keys shown → wrong fingerprint refused → right fingerprints → repository ready with `heads/main` published, `verify` exit 0, the typed keys adopted and no others); `no_adopt` lands under `remotes/` and says so; a tampered archive is refused at the stage and keeps nothing; the SIGKILL test of T1; the injected-failure test at each step; the stage limits and expiry; hostile names (`../`, absolute paths, NUL, overlong, case collisions) in owner/name/metadata refused; the IS-14 table, OpenAPI drift guard, ENF-2 and every earlier check pass; both supply-chain gates reported as they are on a fresh database (they may be red while IS-54 is open; say so), both test runs named, `cargo doc`.

## Prohibited shortcuts

No prefilled fingerprint; no adoption of a key the creator did not type; no `--adopt` without a matching `trust maintainer add` having been recorded for exactly the typed keys; no repository visible before `ready`; no staged archive served or kept after a refusal; no guessed cost constant; no deletion outside the repository root; no hidden/absent/denied difference that a stranger can see; no hard-coded user-visible text.

## Known risks

An archive is hostile input of arbitrary size and shape: every path from upload to import must be bounded and sandboxed, and the creator's typing of fingerprints is the single trust decision — the page must make comparing them easy and skipping them impossible. The start-up sweep deletes directories: its guards (inside the root, a record that says `creating`, logged) are the safety, and its test with a planted link is not optional.

## Required evidence and review-request format

Commits (hash → what, by task); T0's table, fit, worst ratios, the rule with provenance; the transcript of the real-prikk archive path (stage response with keys, wrong and right confirm, result); the kill and failure tests; the HTML of both screens in both languages with the checker's result; the sweep tests including the planted link; every gate with command and exit code and the advisory-database HEAD; deviations and open questions.
